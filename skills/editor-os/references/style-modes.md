# Style modes

Eight edit styles. Each is a **grammar**: what drives the cuts, how long things stay, which transitions exist, what the text does, what the sound is, and the one or two moves that make it recognisable. Distilled from the Edit & Documentary styles of lemo-opuscar; the "built in code" parts are replaced by how to get the same result in ffmpeg, Remotion or any NLE.

Quick chooser: sound-off social → 1 · history/archive → 2 · one real event → 3 · place/process, music-led → 4 · prove a claim → 5 · punchy argument from many sources → 6 · explain one moment → 7 · multi-item bulletin → 8.

Common to all eight: real material is the ground (footage, photos, documents, data); graphics are an overlay system with strict rules; one accent colour; one grade for every source; the sound carries as much as the picture.

---

## 1. Caption-first (social news that reads with the sound off)

**Use for:** stats, news, explainers, Reels/Shorts/TikTok. 16:9 or a 9:16 layout (caption under a footage band, not a crop).

**Idea:** the caption is the shot. Footage is chosen to sit under the line. Voice adds pace, not information.

**Cut grammar**
- Cuts on a 120 BPM half-beat grid (every cut at a multiple of 0.5 s).
- Footage shots 1.5-3 s; data cards and documents 3-4.5 s; a comparison stays until every item has been on screen ≥ 2.5 s.
- Captions cross cuts: picture changes, words don't. A caption changes at most every ~2.5 s even if shots change every 1.5 s. Reading time sets the caption clock, not shot length.
- Order of a fact: footage (feeling) → the number on a card → the document line that says it → the consequence. Never a number without its source tag in the same shot.
- A strong action within 0.5 s of every shot; no frame flat for more than 1 s (rolling counters, line drawing, highlighter sweeps, slow pushes). One held breath (2-3 s) after the turn.

**Transitions (few, fixed):** hard cut (default); 1-2 frame white flash on the turn and on a freeze; card sliding in from the right with speed streaks (0.18 s); split opening from its divider; grid panels rising staggered (0.1 s apart); a 0.3 s punch-in to ~1.25× on the culprit; a mask transition (a photo grows out of a map shape). No dissolves, page turns or 3D flips.

**Text**
- Line split into 2-3 chunks; each chunk slams 0.12 s after the last (scale 1.3 → 1 in 0.13 s with a small overshoot). **One keyword per caption**, on a coloured block that wipes in 0.12 s behind it.
- Second language line under it, smaller, fading in 0.1 s after the first chunk. One line per language, never wrapped; ≤ ~14 CJK characters or ~6-8 English words per caption.
- Source tag slides in 0.2 s whenever a figure appears; footage credit chip in the corner for CC BY shots.
- Numbers: fixed-width digit cells so rolling counters never jitter; counters ease out and change colour when they arrive.
- Colour meaning: one accent = the point/gain; red = loss/spike; everything else neutral.

**Sound:** 120 BPM pulse (kick on the beat, off-beat hats, eighth-note bass, marimba ostinato, brass stabs on big numbers). Hard stop into silence before the turn; turn lands on a major-key hit; half-time and low strings for the threat; a second short silence before the verdict. Foley for every graphic action: chunk slam (click + thump), counter rattle (tick every ~45 ms), whoosh into cards, highlighter squeak, shutter on freeze, stamp thump, riser into a map push. Natural sound of the location J-cut 0.3 s before a freeze.

**Signature moves:** frame-4 hook (largest number lands in the first 0.15 s and rolls to its value) · chunk slam with one keyword · proportion by darkness (a line sweeps across the footage; what it passed goes dark and grey) · the document says it (highlight the actual sentence, then pop the number beside the page) · freeze and question (image stops on the viewer's question, music cut) · ledger split (two counters racing, difference stamped after both settle) · lit grid (three causes, each lighting on its word) · punch and circle the culprit · map pushes into a region, then a photo grows out of its outline.

**Structures:** inverted pyramid with a mid-film turn · list of three that turns on the third · countdown of numbers that ends on the one that matters.

**Pitfalls:** two-line captions in 16:9 read as a slide, split the sentence · voice running up to the cut feels rushed, speed it or shorten · words stronger than the source · blank first frame in split/grid transitions (start them 0.1-0.15 s before the cut) · station logos: crop them, never hide with a caption · chart titles ≤ 6 CJK characters so they can be read in the shot.

## 2. Newsreel (period archive with a fast announcer)

**Use for:** history, anniversaries, "how X took over the world", anything with archive footage and print.

**Idea:** a projected gate (1.37:1 picture, black outside); real footage plus newspapers, maps, magazine pages; a bright announcer over everything; music is a band that edits.

**Cut grammar**
- The band is the clock: hot dance tempo 190-220 BPM; cuts on beats, most on bar lines; quick inserts on beat 3 or the Charleston accent. When a period record plays, cut to the record's own beat, not the grid.
- One story = one place + one kind of material + one line of voice. Stories get shorter towards the end (2-3 bars → 1 bar → beats), then one held button.
- Archive shots 1-2.5 s; print and documents 2.5-4 s (they must be read); comparisons ≥ 2.5 s before the verdict; nothing static for more than ~1 s.
- Archive footage is not given fake camera moves bigger than ~6 %; stills always get a purposeful move.

**Transitions (each once, as punctuation):** iris, diagonal wipe with a bright edge, vertical flip, venetian blinds, clock wipe, spinning paper, page turn, panel push-over, freeze. No dissolves between stories, no whip pans.

**Camera on stills:** spin-and-slam (headline arrives as news) · slow push then punch to the detail · pan across a wide print · tilt to reveal · map push · grid deal (stills land one per beat) → flip-book (series plays as motion) · freeze + jolt on the telling frame · split/quad screen.

**Text:** place-and-date card top-left (`PLACE · DATE`), pulled out in 0.22 s; one item title card per film at most; subtitles on a dark strip, one line per language. Printed pages are never retyped as captions; the announcer and an underline point at them.

**Look:** one silver grade, set per *clip* (never per frame, or footage pumps), gate weave, flicker, a few scratches. Upscale archive ≤ 2.5×.

**Sound:** announcer 6-12 words per line, exclamations, speed ~1.15-1.2, treated as an optical track (band-limited ~260 Hz-3.7 kHz, horn presence, light drive). Band: tuba on 1 and 3, banjo four to the bar, trombone stabs, snare backbeat, honky-tonk piano, muted trumpet melody. If the story has two sides, give the other side its own band and trade bars, then half bars, panned left and right. A period record can take over ("needle drop": click, surface noise, end on its own chord). Projector bed from first to last frame. Two silences: a dead stop before the turn, only the projector before the button.

**Signature moves:** ident ritual (searchlights, globe, fanfare, iris) · spinning paper · map spread with dotted arcs · grid deal → flip-book · document underline word-exact · grease-pencil freeze (hand-drawn ring on the telling frame) · split-screen duel → push-over · needle drop · band stop + button.

**Tone rule:** the jaunty grammar is wrong for disasters and deaths; use a straighter variant (no jokes, still fast).

**Pitfalls:** read the catalogue description before using a film · archive transfers start/end with slates and white flashes, cut inside them · newspapers hold unrelated stories next to yours, crop to the fold · fast announcer outruns subtitles (≤ 12 English words, ≤ 23 CJK characters) · never exceed 2.5× upscale.

## 3. Live countdown (real time, splitting screen)

**Use for:** a launch, a verdict, final whistle, an election night, polls closing, a product keynote's big reveal.

**Idea:** screen time equals event time. One continuous stretch, never re-ordered or sped up. A clock ticks each second; the screen splits into more cells as zero nears, every cell showing the same second; at zero cells merge into one and the clock flips.

**Cut grammar**
- One mapping for the whole film: `M = t − T0`. Each camera has a measured offset baked in once.
- **Cut points are calls.** A layout change starts on the first word of a real call in the native audio, or on a visible event. Music follows; it never sets a cut.
- Cell count rises toward zero: 1 → 2 → 3-4 → many (6-9, clock becomes the centre cell) → **one** at zero → a few again after zero (consequences) → one under the title. The shortest layouts are nearest zero.
- Inside a cell the source cuts itself (a broadcast switching cameras): keep it; you cut only the layout.
- Silence a quarter-second before zero; only native sound and the clock remain.

**Look:** hard rectangles, 8-9 px gutters, camera chip top-left of each cell, orange/accent = now/synced/done, grey = pending. Sync badge top-right of each video cell: synced, "same window, not frame-synced", or "slow motion re-timed". Honesty is part of the look.

**Sound layers:** native sound on top, never ducked · narrator only in gaps, a different voice from the event's · tick every whole second (louder in the last ten) · interface foley on re-layouts · score at 60 BPM so one beat = one second = one tick: drone whose low-pass opens toward zero, bass drum per beat, timpani roll into zero, silence, then a major chord at zero. Hot peaks from broadcast audio need a pre-limit near −4 dBFS.

**Signature moves:** many into one (cells fly to their tile of the full frame with a flash) · clock inversion at zero · control strip (who has control; active segment lights on the call) · grid fills on the spoken count · planned vs heard checklist · second countdown to the next milestone · event flag · punch-in on a call (extract at higher resolution from the same source).

**Pitfalls:** unsyncable footage: never fake sync, label it · measure zero with two independent marks (a visible flash and the spoken word); metadata lies; re-measure on the file you actually cut (`-c copy` starts on the previous keyframe) · picture and audio of one file can disagree by ~1 s · a static wide shot for ten seconds reads flat: give each 1-2 s a new element · a whole-sentence caption spoils the end of a count: split into cues · proportional digits make the clock jitter.

## 4. Time-lapse symphony (score first, no narrator)

**Use for:** a city, port, airport, market, factory, a river over a year; mood pieces; openers.

**Idea:** cut like a piece of minimalist music. Time-lapse turns traffic and crowds into rivers of light; cuts get denser with the score's subdivisions; one thing is slowed almost to a stop; end on a hard cut to black on a downbeat. Few words, each earned.

**Cut grammar (score decides)**

| Score texture | Cut length | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Opening hit + eighth arpeggio | 2 bars → 1 bar | opening still, first single-camera time-lapse |
| Eighths | 2 beats → 1 beat | dawn, match cuts |
| Triplets against eighths | 1 beat → ½ beat | morning, montage of streets |
| Sixteenths, full ensemble | 1-4 frames, grid cells re-cut each sixteenth | the grid |
| Arpeggios stop, one long chord | 3-4 s, one or two shots | the brake: the slowed thing |
| Sixteenths + triplets + horns | ½ beat → 4 → 2 frames | dusk to night, reverse flashback |
| Dry cut of all instruments, reverb ringing | hard cut to black on the bar line | the hard stop |

- All cut times come from one function of (bar, beat); never type shot lengths in seconds.
- **One long single-camera time-lapse is the clock** (≥ 12 h): it returns 3-4 times so the viewer knows the time without a caption.
- Density follows the score: never cut faster than the current subdivision; slowest picture under the place where arpeggios stop.
- Shape match cuts on the beat (clock → clock, arch → arch). Transitions: hard cuts only, plus the cut to black.

**Time handling:** speed is the instrument (0.3× to 75×). Speeds above 1× use **frame blending** (average the source frames each output frame covers; ~40 % shutter looks better than 100 %). Speed ramps are smooth (smoothstep) and always mean something: a corridor accelerates out of real time; a fast shot brakes to 0.3× on the bar where the music stops.

**Text:** left-aligned, never centred, never on a box (soft darkening behind instead). Number cards (2-4 at most): big number, rule growing under it, one line saying what it counts and the source year; digits lock one per sixteenth, one glockenspiel note per lock. One closing quote from a public-domain text.

**Sound:** music only. No narrator, no source sound, no effects. Additive writing: eighths → triplets over eighths → sixteenths. The *break* is all arpeggios stopping at once into one held chord; the return is a full hit; the hard stop is a dry cut of every instrument in ~15 ms with the reverb tail left ringing; the coda is one soft unresolved chord with the opening figure returning.

**Signature moves:** light river (frame blending) · speed ramp · brake into slow motion · crowd → tracks (fixed camera only) · grid 1×2 → 2×2 → 3×3 → 4×4 · counterpoint split (archive vs today) · day/night flicker every 2 frames · reverse flashback in the last bar · hard stop.

**Pitfalls:** watermarks/slates hidden in CC footage (check a 1-second contact sheet of every source) · hidden jump cuts inside hyperlapse sources make ghosts under blending (scene-detect every source) · grey-world white balance kills night, use strength 0 · tracking needs a fixed camera · motion-compensated slow motion tears thin lines (use crossfade) · don't make an identifiable ordinary person the slowed hero · don't end on dead black: page → quote over one last moving shot → end card.

## 5. Investigation (sources meet at one moment)

**Use for:** "what really happened" pieces: accidents, disputed calls, launch failures, protests, market crashes.

**Idea:** footage, photos, documents, sound, maps from one event on one shared clock so they check each other. Payoff of each section: two or more independent sources pointing at the same place or second. **Cut points are sync points.**

**Grammar**
- One finding per layout. At most five sources on a board.
- Time is carried by readouts in the picture: timestamp on each card, playhead on a chart, a stopwatch over the decisive seconds. The scale changes at section cuts (hours → minutes → seconds → days). A permanent timeline strip is usually wrong.
- Uncertainty is drawn: `≈`, "inferred", dashed boxes. Known and unknown are separate columns. A source that doesn't cover a stretch simply isn't on screen yet; never an empty "no footage" box.
- Colours: sources greyscale, **one accent that only ever points** (circles, playhead, sync lines, highlights).
- Cut in with a 3-4 frame white flash as if pulled from an evidence locker; nothing flies or bounces. Only *drawn* things are progressive: circles drawn clockwise in 0.5 s, leaders 0.4-0.8 s, highlights wiped, typewriter text for telegrams.
- Something changes at least every second; holds are for verdicts.

**Sound:** low sustained bed + room tone ~30 dB under voice; a pendulum tick every 0.5 s in minute-scale sections, one tick per *event* second in a timed run (the audible stopwatch); pizzicato ostinato for slow sections, tremolo clusters for tension; original sound band-passed (180 Hz-5.5 kHz) with a waveform and word-by-word lighting, music ducked ~12 dB under it, original sound and score alternate (never both loud); calm flat voice; foley is paper, shutter, Morse, teletype, pencil. One deep thump for the moment of impact, then near-silence. No score that tells you how to feel; no slow motion of catastrophe.

**Signature moves:** freeze, circle, magnify (loupe cropped from the same pixels, never beyond 1 source px per screen px) · hard sync point (impact, flash, public clock, sound peak) · three-column sync grid filling left to right as the clock runs · rewind cold open (start on the decisive frame, build the grid, rewind) · telegrams on the clock · chart with playhead · map → plan · evidence → model links converging · then-vs-record (period claim as sound, then the fact-check, wrong words struck) · known/unresolved · dots for people · evidence map as the poster frame.

**Pitfalls:** inference passed as fact · early reports as facts · faking a multi-camera event · seams in "continuous" shots · time zones (state one once) · too many words on screen · catastrophe as spectacle.

## 6. Montage (beat-machine, collision)

**Use for:** construction/engineering histories, labour and collective effort, mass events, anniversaries, hype pieces, any "many people, one rhythm" story.

**Idea:** real material printed as one two-tone sheet with a single red; diagonal bands carry type; windows hold the film; people are cut out and stacked into structures; two images collide to make a third idea (A + B → concept).

**Cut grammar**
- Everything on a 120 BPM grid (beat 0.5 s, bar 2 s) or the score's tempo. Footage shots 0.5-2 s, one idea per shot.
- **Acceleration by halving:** 0.5 → 0.25 → 0.125 s, never shorter than 3 frames. Holds are reserved for reading.
- **Cut rule:** adjacent shots must conflict in at least one of: direction (left slam / right slam), value (light ground / dark ground), geometry (diagonal / upright). Never two paper documents in a row.
- Nothing static for more than a second; inside a hold something moves.
- **Two silences:** one where the orchestra stops and only a real recording carries the cut; one true half-bar of nothing (a dark frame, one dot) before the heaviest hit.

**Transitions inside the medium:** red band wipe (260 px at 72°, 0.25 s, lands on the downbeat) · iris from a circle drawn on a map or photo · slam from outside along the axis in 0.08-0.2 s with a frame shake (amplitude 3-18 px, decaying) · flash cut (one frame of 50-80 % white) · hard cut between opposite grounds · jump-in (inside a freeze, a cut to a 1.2× crop).

**Text:** type is structure: on a −18° band, as solid blocks, or stacked. Never floating captions, never centred, never a header. Numbers can be bigger than the words. Every archive window carries a footage tag; every fact a source tag.

**Sound:** machine ostinato in eighths (spiccato cellos + staccato bassoon), staccato tuba/trombone on the beat, snare sixteenth taps, **xylophone for counts (one rising note per lit cell / built floor)**, brass stabs, timpani rolls into section changes, bass drum + gong on the heaviest hit. Original sound alternates with music, brought in a beat early (J-cut). Foley for every printed action (slam = thump + clank; stamp = thump + clack; wipe = whoosh; marker = filtered noise scrub; paper = band-passed rustle). Fade the first 25 ms of the master so a hit at sample 0 doesn't overshoot.

**Signature moves:** the document is marked, not quoted (red bar on the actual sentence, then the number as a block) · the document becomes the chart (drawings lifted off the page and stood to scale) · A + B → idea (two kinds of image alternate faster and faster until a card states what the collision meant) · count the many (a counter driven by cuts, a grid filling, a tower floor by floor) · people build the structure (cut-outs stacked) · silence, then the heaviest hit.

**Pitfalls:** upscaled archive on a full 1080p frame turns to mush, window it · per-frame grading pumps · red on red · a face as hero · counters read as facts: only end values and dated anchors are facts, and a caption beside a counter must match the number shown at every frame · a number without a unit · short labels inside an acceleration fail reading time by design: keep text out of the 1/8-beat cuts and put the result in a held block after.

## 7. Telestrator (play, rewind, freeze, draw)

**Use for:** sports analysis, disputed decisions, any single decisive moment that needs "look here". Also screen-recording explainers and product walkthroughs.

**Idea:** every piece of action is seen moving before it is explained. Freeze, draw on the real picture, play on. **One freeze, one finding**, with one label and at most two groups of lines. The same few seconds are reused, each pass going one layer deeper.

**Order inside one piece of action:** live at 1× (original sound) → rewind (0.6 s, reversed with tracking bars and ◀◀) → freeze ① → slow play (0.6-0.85×, frame-blended) → freeze ② → slow play with a stopwatch → freeze ③ → ball/object flight with a comet trail → another angle (replay).
- Lighter variants for secondary moments: **freeze-in-play** (no rewind, 1× up to the decisive pass, freeze, draw, play on) and **dual-angle freeze** (both angles play side by side and arrive at the same instant).
- Freeze length 1.8-2.4 s (longer needs a second action inside it); 2-4 freezes per piece of action; more than four, split with a replay.
- Between segments only the packaging wipe; inside a segment, hard cuts.

**Drawing:** freeze entry = white flash 0.1 s + punch 1.035 → 1.0 in 0.35 s + desaturate ~40 % + heavier vignette. Draw order in a freeze: ring (who) → area or link (what happened) → label (the one-line finding), 0.3-0.6 s apart. Overlays use the same transform as the picture or they slide off the subject. Trails in screen space keep only the last ~0.6 s (the camera pans).

**Colour identity (the one rule that matters):** one colour = one identity for the whole film. Followed subject / ball / main line = yellow; opponents = cyan; uncovered space = red; neutral marks = chalk white.

**Data counted from the footage:** times from frame counts (stopwatch over source frames), distances from a perspective transform fitted to visible markings. Anything the footage can't measure (speed, distance run) isn't stated. Where a ball is invisible, interpolate and mark "≈".

**Sound:** voice-over one line per freeze (~2.5-3 s). **On a freeze the drums stop**: one brass stab + timpani + low thump + shutter, then only closed hi-hat; the music freezes with the picture. Foley: marker squeak for each line, pops for rings, tick per landed label, tape-motor whine on rewind, stopwatch tick every 0.2 s, stamp thump once per film.

**Pitfalls:** tracking points read off a scaled contact sheet are 20-50 px off, read on full-resolution crops and verify with a debug overlay · overlays not under the punch transform · packaging late by one frame shows the logo (bugs never fade in over footage) · score state must match the moment · names in TTS spelled phonetically for the voice, real spelling in subtitles · freeze labels live ~1 s; anything the viewer must read gets ≥ 2.5 s.

## 8. Broadcast package (a bulletin with a system)

**Use for:** weekly round-ups, product/market/news digests, multi-item "state of X" videos, company updates.

**Idea:** the style is the **package system plus its cutting rules**. Every graphic is built from the same few parts so viewers read the system before the words.

**The bulletin's order:** sting → rundown ("today we have four stories") → item → item → … → back to the rundown, ticked off → sign-off. Each item opens with its category on the strap's tab and its date on the first graphic.

**Grammar**
- 120 BPM grid, beat 0 at the end of the sting. Every cut and wipe on a beat or half-beat.
- Sting 1.5 s (a hit within 0.4 s); rundown 4-5 s; story shots 3-7 s; a map or explainer long take (6-12 s) as the bulletin's breath; comparisons stay until every item has been fully on screen ≥ 2.5 s.
- **A package action every 0.5-1 s** inside a shot (flip, count, stamp, tick, strap change, map light). No frame flat > 1 s; a strong action within 0.5 s of every cut.
- **The strap holds the headline** and never leaves mid-programme; words change with a vertical flip; the tab flashes white when the category changes. No separate item cards between stories.
- Order inside an item: what (name/number) → how big (line, bars, price board) → who it hits or who said it → verdict stamp. One fact per screen. Date every story.
- Transitions: vertical band wipe between stories; hard cut + timpani inside a story; nothing else.
- **The package layer travels with its picture:** under a wipe each side draws its own strap words, tab, source line; they never lag the picture by 0.3-0.5 s.
- Clean frame: no crawl, no burned-in subtitles; subtitles ship as a separate `.srt`.

**Package parts:** brand plate that is the sting, the bug, the desk front and the end card · rundown (rows flip in on the beat, ticked at the end) · split-flap before/after boards · threshold chart (line advances one period per step toward a dashed threshold; music stops one beat before the last step; the crossing gets the heaviest hit) · explainer map in one long take · documents flying out of a city · quote card with the official words (never paraphrase inside quotation marks) · spec card with proportion bars on one scale · stamps (one per story at most).

**Sound:** one anchor voice, one sentence per graphic, starting 0.1 s after the cut; news theme 120 BPM minor (spiccato violins, pizzicato bass, horn pads, kick on 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4); sections change mood (markets: tremolo and four-on-the-floor heartbeat; policy: pizzicato and horns only); glockenspiel as rundown rows land; timpani on hard cuts; one beat of silence before the biggest break then the heaviest hit; held drone after the verdict stamp; major-key lift for the last story; tutti on the end card.

**Pitfalls:** package drawn once on top of everything so the strap catches up late · removing subtitles without re-laying the page leaves a dead band · masked type peeking in · words stronger than the source ("request for information" ≠ "investigation") · thresholds depend on the reference ("bear market" from which high?) · attribute what only a report says · a schematic posing as data · gold on paper has no contrast · stamps over key words mask the voice.

## Translating a mode to tools

| Need | ffmpeg / NLE |
|---|---|
| Cut on a grid | `scripts/editgrid.py snap` for times; trim/concat list from `ffmpeg-edit-toolkit` |
| Frame blending for time-lapse | `tmix=frames=N` / `minterpolate` (mci for soft subjects) / `setpts=PTS/k` |
| Speed ramp | `setpts` with an expression over `T`, or piecewise segments joined |
| Punch-in | `zoompan` or `scale+crop` with eased expression |
| Freeze frame | `trim` one frame + `loop` / `tpad=stop_mode=clone` |
| Rewind | `reverse` filter on the short span + noise/jitter overlay |
| Colour grade per clip | one LUT (`lut3d`) or `eq`/`colorbalance` applied identically to all |
| Captions with chunks | Remotion (`remotion-captions`) or ASS subtitles with per-chunk `\t` transforms |
| Sound placement | `adelay` per event from the cue sheet; mix with `amix`/`sidechaincompress` |
| Loudness | two-pass `loudnorm` to −14 LUFS, TP −1.2 |
| 9:16 delivery | `reel-export` |
