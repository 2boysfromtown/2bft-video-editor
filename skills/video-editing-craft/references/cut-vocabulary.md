# Cut vocabulary: what each cut is for

Pick a cut for what it says to the viewer, not because it looks clever. Commands are in `ffmpeg-edit-toolkit/references/recipes.md`.

| Cut | What it is | Use it to | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Straight cut** | Instant switch, picture and sound together | Move on cleanly; the default. Most cuts should be this | Nothing; it is the baseline against which other cuts mean something |
| **Jump cut** | Remove a slice from one shot so it jumps in time | Cut pauses, mistakes and filler from talking heads; show time passing; comedy | Too many in a calm scene feels frantic; vary shot size slightly to soften |
| **Cut on action** | Cut while something moves (a turn, a stand, a swing) so shot A's out-point matches shot B's in-point | Make cuts feel smooth and natural; keep the eye on the action | Match direction and speed of the movement |
| **J-cut** | Next shot's sound starts before its picture | Lead into a new scene or location; tell the viewer what's coming; hide a cut | Needs sound from before the picture in-point |
| **L-cut** | Previous shot's sound continues over the next picture | Conversations: show the listener while the speaker keeps talking; let a line linger over B-roll | Needs sound after the out-point |
| **Cutaway** | Cut to something else in the scene (not always a face) | Show where we are, illustrate what's said, cover a jump cut | Must be relevant; a reaction shot is a cutaway of a face |
| **Cross-cutting** | Alternate between two things happening at once | Build suspense; connect two places (phone calls, chases, heists) | Keep both lines readable; accelerate as they converge |
| **Montage** | A run of short shots over time | Training, preparation, time passing, change in a character | Needs a clear goal and an ending beat |
| **Match cut** | Two shots matched on shape, colour, movement or sound | Link two ideas or places elegantly; transform one thing into another | Three kinds: graphic, movement, audio. Plan it at the shoot if you can |
| **Smash cut** | Abrupt cut between contrasting picture and sound | Shock, punchline, quiet after loud | Don't soften it with a transition; its force is the abruptness |
| **Fade (to black / to white)** | Picture fades to a colour | Start or end a story or section; white often = dream or death | Overuse makes everything feel like an ending |
| **Dissolve** | One shot fades into the next | Passing time within or between scenes | A dissolve is a statement about time; don't use it as filler |
| **Cut to reaction** | Cut to someone responding | Land a joke, show emotion, give the audience a reality check | Hold the face for exactly as long as the beat needs (see `comedy-editing-timing`) |

## Quick picks

- Talking head full of pauses: jump cuts, then cutaways or punch-ins to hide the rhythm.
- Conversation: L-cuts and J-cuts so the dialogue flows over the picture changes.
- Two-place tension: cross-cut and raise CPM as it builds.
- Section change: music cue + smash cut or full-screen transition, not a random wipe.
- Anything abstract: cut to motion graphics instead of a still face.

Source: Shutterstock Tutorials ("9 Cuts Every Video Editor Should Know") and Jonny Duncan ("10 Editing Cuts Every Video Editor Should Know"), summarised.
